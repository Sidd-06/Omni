Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Have questions about how OmniDownloader works, its safety, legal compliance, or supported video resolutions? Read our detailed FAQ guide below.

General Questions

What is OmniDownloader?

OmniDownloader is a local, lightweight download controller utility. It provides a simple, clean, and adware-free web dashboard to download and archive digital media files locally. By running directly on your computer, it offers maximum speed, security, and full respect for user privacy.

How does it download media?

When you paste a link, the backend runs a subprocess parsing metadata from the platform. Once you select a quality option, the server connects to the raw content delivery networks (CDNs), streams the download segments, post-processes them (e.g. converting WebM audio to MP3 or merging HD video and audio), and then hands the completed file directly to your browser's download manager.

Is the downloader safe to use?

Absolutely. Public download portals are notorious for hiding redirects, browser hijackers, and pop-up ads. Since OmniDownloader is served locally on your own computer, it contains zero trackers, zero malware, and does not sell or store your browsing data. All execution is self-contained.

Technical & Format Questions

What is the difference between MP4, MP3, and M4A?

  • MP4: The most compatible container format for video and audio. Works on virtually every smartphone, tablet, computer, and smart TV.
  • MP3: The standard compressed format for audio. Ideal for offline listening on audio players. Our backend uses local high-quality encoders to extract audio streams and encode them to 320kbps MP3s.
  • M4A: An AAC-encoded audio format. It is the raw audio format streamed by video servers. Downloading M4A is faster because it does not require any CPU-heavy audio conversion.

Why does downloading a 1080p Full HD video take a moment before appearing?

Unlike standard 360p or 480p videos which are served as pre-merged files, high-definition streams (720p, 1080p, and above) are hosted on video servers as separate video-only and audio-only streams to save bandwidth. To compile a 1080p video, OmniDownloader must download both files to a temporary server directory, execute our local FFmpeg binary to stitch them together, and then output the file. Our interface features a green pulsing badge and progress indicators during this process so you know exactly what the server is doing.

What does the 'Parallel Stream Acceleration' badge mean?

Our downloader backend uses multi-threaded download acceleration. By utilizing 5 concurrent threads, we split large media files into smaller fragments, download them simultaneously, and stitch them back together. This speeds up downloads up to 5 times, helping you bypass bandwidth throttling enforced by some platforms.

Copyright & Legal Policies

Is it legal to use OmniDownloader?

Yes. Providing a media player or download wrapper is entirely legal. However, the legality of downloading files depends on the nature of the media and your usage:

  • Permissible Uses: Downloading your own uploaded videos, downloading public-domain historical content, downloading Creative Commons licensed clips, or extracting brief snippets for scholarly criticism and fair use.
  • Prohibited Uses: Downloading copyright-protected commercial material, music videos, private profiles, or re-distributing someone else's media without their license or permission.

We advise all users to review platform licenses and comply with intellectual property laws before downloading.

Do you store my downloaded files?

Never. OmniDownloader is designed around data minimisation. Our server maintains zero databases. Any files created in our local temp/ folder during format merging are deleted immediately the moment your web browser finished retrieving the download.

Does the downloader work on private accounts?

No. OmniDownloader does not ask for or store credentials, meaning it cannot access or extract media from private Instagram accounts, private YouTube uploads, or paid content structures. It only interacts with publicly accessible URLs.
